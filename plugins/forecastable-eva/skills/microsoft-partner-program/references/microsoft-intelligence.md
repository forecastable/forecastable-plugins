# Microsoft (Azure and the Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program) partner program intelligence
Version: 2026.09 (built 2026-09-26). Refreshed monthly. Facts carry source tags; see Sources.

Scope: Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program (MAICPP, formerly Microsoft Partner Network, MPN), Partner Center, Solutions Partner designations, specializations, Microsoft Marketplace (the merged AppSource and Azure Marketplace), Azure IP co-sell, services co-sell, MACC, Frontier Accelerate (FY27 incentives and offers framework), Frontier Accelerate for Marketplace (the FY27 software company offer that replaced ISV Success, Marketplace Rewards, Azure IP co-sell enrollment and certified software designation enrollment), CSP touchpoints that matter to ISVs and services firms.

Reading rules for agents: Microsoft's fiscal year runs July 1 to June 30. "FY27" means July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027 [M13]. Microsoft rewrote several program names in 2026 with a find-and-replace on older blog posts, so some 2024 and 2025 blog text now says "Frontier Accelerate for Marketplace" where the original said "ISV Success" or "Marketplace Rewards" (see section 14). Trust dated Microsoft Learn pages and Partner Center announcements over blog posts when they conflict.

## The twelve things that matter right now

1. **FY27 co-sell is Marketplace-first, and PRACR is gone as the scale mechanism.** From July 1, 2026, Partner Reported Azure Consumed Revenue (PRACR) "no longer operates as a broad co-sell mechanism"; Marketplace is "the primary path for co-sell at scale" and co-sell credit for Marketplace transactions is recognized through Marketplace Billed Sales (MBS) [M13]. Partners with scenarios that cannot transact through Marketplace are told to work out a path with their Partner Development Manager (PDM) [M13]. Independent coverage describes a one-year transition for qualified ISVs [M98]. Practical meaning: an ISV that sells Azure-hosted SaaS direct and only reports consumption loses most of its Microsoft seller pull in FY27.

2. **Frontier Accelerate for Marketplace replaced four ISV programs in September 2026.** It consolidates ISV Success, Marketplace Rewards, Azure IP co-sell and Solutions Partner with certified software designations into one offer with a Build and Publish, Grow, Differentiate journey; GA was planned for September 4, 2026 [M30], with Microsoft briefings saying "late September" [M94]. Two paths: no-cost, and Premium at a list price of USD 1,500 per year (USD 1,549.80 shown on the overview page, plus tax) [M22][M30]. Enrollment is now a prerequisite for any new co-sell listing, for pursuing a certified software designation, for Marketplace go-to-market benefits and for partner incentives [M28][M29][M30]. ISV Success members migrate to Premium automatically; Marketplace Rewards users, CSD holders and active Azure IP co-sell partners migrate to the no-cost path [M30][M94].

3. **Azure IP co-sell eligible is still the gate to everything that moves Microsoft sellers.** Requirements: co-sell ready plus USD 100,000 trailing-12-month Azure Consumed Revenue or Marketplace Billed Sales (Azure credits and ACO excluded), Azure technical validation (primarily platformed on Azure), a reference architecture diagram (mandatory for SaaS and Dragon Copilot only), and a transactable offer (required for new offers since July 11, 2023) [M1]. Emerging FY27 criteria from a pilot add a third route: a MACC of USD 1M or more, alongside USD 100K MBS or USD 100K attributable ACR [M27][M30]. Eligible offers get the "Microsoft preferred solutions" badge, which can take up to 30 days to show [M2].

4. **MACC drawdown is the commercial hook: 100 percent, dollar for dollar, no cap.** Purchases of Azure IP co-sell eligible offers through Marketplace count 100 percent toward the customer's Azure consumption commitment [M36][M72][M79]; Microsoft calls it a "dollar-for-dollar, no limit match" that no other hyperscaler offers [M94]. It applies to private offers, multiparty private offers and resale enabled offers of eligible solutions, but not to CSP private offers [M36][M40]. It is enrolled per offer (each offer must be MACC-eligible), excludes free and BYOL offers, and excludes licenses used on-premises or hybrid; enrollment follows IP co-sell eligibility within one week [M43]. Microsoft states over 85 percent of Marketplace customers have Azure commitments [M94] and that 80 percent of customers with commitments buy from Marketplace [M81].

5. **The fee is 3 percent, and 1.5 percent on renewals if you attest correctly.** Microsoft keeps a 3 percent standard store service fee on transact offers [M34]. A 50 percent discount on that fee applies to customer private offer and multiparty private offer renewals, including migrating an existing off-Marketplace contract or upselling an existing paid customer; you must self-attest renewal status at private offer creation, it cannot be fixed retroactively, and private offers created before October 1, 2024 are not eligible [M42]. In MPOs the fee is charged to the software company on the partner price it sets, not on the channel partner's markup [M39].

6. **Channel-led Marketplace went global in 2026.** Multiparty private offers (MPO) now cover customers in 37 countries: the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, 30 European countries (from May 27, 2026) and Australia, Japan and South Africa (from July 15 to 16, 2026) [M13][M17][M38][M78]. Resale enabled offers (REO), where an ISV authorizes a channel partner to create private offers on its behalf, are available globally except Belarus, Brazil, China, India, Mexico, Russia, Singapore and South Korea [M37]. Microsoft says further MPO markets roll out through FY27 and FY28 [M93]. Rule of thumb from Microsoft's channel team: if the customer buys Azure through CSP, neither MPO nor REO applies; if the customer is on EA or MCA-E (direct or indirect EA), MPO or REO applies [M93].

7. **Deal registration mechanics decide whether an ISV gets credit.** A co-sell deal registers only if it is co-sell or partner-led, at least USD 25,000, on a Microsoft-managed account, includes an Azure IP co-sell eligible solution, and is marked Won; you have 60 days from contract signature to register, and should wait 72 hours after creating the deal before marking Won [M6]. Solution Area and Solution Play are mandatory for new IP co-sell deals [M7]; the Marketplace Intent field (Yes, No, Have not decided) became mandatory for API and connector submissions on January 5, 2026 [M21]. The Qualified Referral Program (QRP) was retired at the end of March 2026; all referrals now run through Partner Center Referrals, and shared referrals land in the Leads tab as SMB opportunities [M18].

8. **Solutions Partner designations: same math, new badge names, harder renewals coming.** Qualification is a partner capability score of at least 70 of 100 with at least one point in every metric, measured on any day in a rolling six-month window [M47]. Since August 2026 the customer-facing badges map to three solution areas: Solutions Partner for AI Business Solutions (Business Applications, Modern Work), Cloud & AI Platforms (Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure) and Security; the six pathways, scoring and benefits are unchanged [M14][M46]. The annual fee is USD 4,875 [M54][M55]. From early 2027 renewal must be completed by the anniversary date (window opens 30 days before, the 30-day post-anniversary grace period ends) [M13]. On July 31, 2026 Microsoft removed the ACR threshold from the SMB track for the three Azure pathways: 80 percent of customers in SMB or SMC-C segments now qualifies you [M13].

9. **Azure Expert MSP is being retired; the Frontier Partner specialization is the successor.** No new Azure Expert MSP enrollments from September 15, 2026; renewals end January 2027; eligible engagements continue under Frontier Accelerate for Azure through June 30, 2027 [M53]. The Frontier Partner badge also retires at the end of June 2027; Microsoft points partners to the new Frontier Partner specialization, "available in Partner Center soon in FY27" [M14][M71]. The EA-to-CSP-for-Azure transfer tool, previously an Azure Expert MSP perk, opened on July 7, 2026 to partners holding a Cloud & AI Platforms designation plus CSP direct bill authorization [M13].

10. **FY27 incentives are organized under "Frontier Accelerate", and the detailed guide is behind login.** Frontier Accelerate is Microsoft's unified FY27 customer value framework with three pillars (Trusted Expertise, Savings and Investments, Skilled Teams), with variants for Azure, Security, AI Business Solutions and Marketplace [M71][M85][M86]. It absorbed FY26's Azure Accelerate (itself the July 2025 consolidation of Azure Migrate and Modernize, Azure Innovate and Cloud Accelerate Factory) [M84][M105]. Cloud Accelerate Factory (zero-cost, Microsoft-delivered deployment help across 30+ Azure services) is now available to partners with eligible Azure specializations, not only through nominated engagements [M13]. Official software-company funding reference points: CMMP (Customer Migration and Modernization Program) up to USD 275,000 per eligible engagement [M28]. The FY27 Commercial Partner Incentives Guide lives at incentiveguide.partner.microsoft.com and requires sign-in [M97].

11. **Azure sponsorship is now the biggest deal-closing lever for Marketplace ISVs, and it scales with MBS.** Under Frontier Accelerate for Marketplace, Grow-stage allowances go up to USD 400,000 for Marketplace adoption grants plus up to USD 25,000 for free-trial costs; Differentiate (certified software designation holders at top MBS tiers) goes up to USD 2.1M total (USD 2M adoption, USD 100K trials); MPO channel partners can earn up to USD 200,000 [M94]. Grant rules: max USD 200K per request; 90-day validity under USD 100K, 180 days at or above; capped at 10 percent of ACV for 3+ year deals or 3 percent of TCV for 1 to 2 year deals; customer account team approval at USD 35K or more; request only after the deal closes and within 60 days; one grant per deal; not usable on CSP billing accounts; sponsored usage does not count toward ACR or MACC [M31].

12. **Marketplace mechanics improved materially in 2026; most ISVs have not turned them on.** New since May 2026: SaaS auto activation (billing starts at purchase, on by default for new plans) [M17]; App Advisor AI listing feedback [M17]; custom contract lengths from 1 to 120 months for SaaS and professional services private offers (July 6) [M13][M34]; expanded trials of 1 to 180 days across SaaS, VM, Dynamics 365 and Power BI (July 24) [M13]; a "Request private offer" button on listings that is off by default (July 20) [M13]; intelligent AI discovery and purchase order mapping (September) [M15][M91]. Microsoft also says "Partner Center for Sales", an AI-assisted co-sell experience, launches later in FY27 [M71].

### 1.3 Why a Microsoft seller cares (compensation and quota)

- **What Microsoft states publicly.** "Our global field sellers are aligned to the marketplace and incentivized to co-sell with you... helping Microsoft sellers retire quota" and IP co-sell eligible sales "can help Microsoft sellers earn quota credit and fulfill customer MACCs" [M79]. In the September 2026 FAM briefing Microsoft said the field has been "intentionally aligned so that sellers are incentivized to sell through the marketplace," that there is a seller incentive for co-sell eligible Marketplace solutions and a separate incentive for designated (CSD) solutions, and that details are not public [M94]. The Microsoft for Startups Azure IP Co-sell Acceleration page lists "Sellers receive quota retirement to co-sell your solution" [M69].
- **The MACC mechanism is the customer-side motive.** Every eligible Marketplace dollar retires the customer's Azure commitment 100 percent, which helps the account team hit consumption commitments without new Azure workloads [M72][M94].
- **Exact quota credit percentages are not published.** Older program guides (pre-2024) described Microsoft sellers receiving quota credit equal to 10 percent of annual contract value on IP co-sell deals [UNVERIFIED, historical program guide, no current source]. Vendor claim: AEs get "quota relief" once an ISV passes USD 100K MBS [M107, VENDOR, undated]. The FY27 Azure IP co-sell program guide in the official resources collection is JavaScript-rendered and could not be retrieved [M88]; ask your PDM or Engagement Manager for the current seller incentive one-pager.
- **Practical implication.** The seller-facing pitch is: (1) the deal retires MACC, (2) it counts as a co-sell win credited through MBS, (3) your solution is IP co-sell eligible or CSD (seller incentive), (4) you bring a qualified, specific ask. Microsoft's own advice is to tell the seller "how much quota credit they'll receive" in the first note after a referral is accepted [M79].

### 1.4 How the field finds you

- Co-sell ready solutions appear in Microsoft internal catalogs and seller tools; Azure IP co-sell eligible and CSD solutions get "prioritized visibility in our seller tools" [M3][M94].
- Microsoft sellers match inbound referrals to partners using your published Partner Center business profile, response history and accept or decline patterns; responding fast and accepting only good-fit deals increases future visibility [M8][M9].
- Partner-matching was modernized with "real-time data and AI logic" when QRP was retired [M18].

### 2.2 Account structure, linking and identifiers

- Enrolling in MAICPP creates a PGA and a PLA by default. Find both in Partner Center > Settings (gear) > Account settings > Identifiers > Microsoft AI Cloud Partner Program tab (type "PartnerGlobal" for the PGA) [M62][M64].
- Use location (PLA) IDs, not the PGA, for incentive and CSP associations [M64].
- CSP direct bill authorization checks: MAICPP enrollment and the security requirement are validated per tenant (since August 2026, enforcement 90 days after release); TTM revenue of USD 1M or more, active support plan and an SPD are validated at PGA level; view in Settings > Account settings > Eligibility Dashboard [M14].
- The co-sell connector requires that the PartnerID on the Partner Center tenant matches the PartnerID of the co-sell solution [M11].
- Attribution links that drive your capability score and incentives: PAL for Azure plan (MCA) subscriptions, DPOR for EA subscriptions, CSP Tier 1 and Tier 2 for CSP; CPOR for Dynamics, Power Platform and some Modern Work metrics [M48][M49][M61].

### 4.2 Solutions Partner designations (SPD): general rules

- Three badges, six pathways: Cloud & AI Platforms (Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure), AI Business Solutions (Business Applications, Modern Work), Security [M46].

For Data & AI, Digital & App Innovation, Infrastructure and Business Applications you are auto-classified; for Modern Work and Security Microsoft scores both and uses the higher [M47][M50][M51]. Azure pathways: SMB if 80 percent or more of customers are SMB (ACR threshold removed July 31, 2026) [M48][M13].
- Legacy Gold and Silver partners qualifying for a pathway are auto-enrolled without buying; already-enrolled partners get new pathways auto-added when they qualify [M47].
- Sovereign and isolated clouds (Azure China, US Gov) are not counted [M48].

### 5.5 Private offers (direct to customer)

- Created in Partner Center > Marketplace offers > Private offers > Customers, using the customer's Azure billing account ID (only the customer can see it; they find it in Azure portal Cost Management + Billing) [M35][M39].
- Supported offer types: SaaS, professional services, VM, container, Azure application, Dragon Copilot; hardware not allowed [M35].
- Mechanics: time-bound pricing with accept-by date and end date; attach a PDF contract (one document, up to 3 MB); send a link; available within 15 minutes (private plans take up to 48 hours); bundles of up to 10 plans; target an organization, not a tenant [M35][M39].

- Customer acceptance does not start billing; the customer must purchase (subscribe or deploy); for SaaS they must configure within 30 days of subscribing [M39].
- Accepted offers cannot be changed; before acceptance you can withdraw, edit and resubmit [M39].
- Private plans (the older construct) are made available to specific Azure subscriptions, take up to 48 hours, and max three-year duration [M34][M35].
- "Request private offer" toggle on the listing (July 20, 2026): for SaaS, Azure application, container and VM offers; off by default; requests flow through Marketplace leads, APIs and CRM connectors [M13].
- APIs: Private Offer APIs cover direct, CSP, multiparty and resale-enabled authorizations [M108][M39].

### 6.4 Deal registration (IP co-sell)

Eligibility (all required): deal type co-sell or partner-led; value of at least USD 25,000; Microsoft-managed customer account; at least one Azure IP co-sell eligible solution; status Won; for co-sell type, Microsoft accepted the invitation or marked Won [M6].

Timing: register within 60 days of contract signature (contract signed date can be at most 60 days in the past and on or after referral creation or Microsoft invitation, whichever is later); contract start date up to 90 days in the past; wait 72 hours between creating the deal and marking Won [M6].

Form essentials: Is Marketplace transaction (Yes enables simplified, automated Marketplace validation) and estimated transaction date; deployed on Azure (required; "Other" closes without registration); perpetual contract (TCV spread over six years; ACV must exceed USD 25,000); TCV (software and services, no hardware); solution value; tenant type (customer or partner; change allowed only within 90 days and with evidence); pricing model (PAYG goes to manual review) [M6].

States: Registration pending > Approved or Review pending > Review passed or failed or Action required > Marketplace validation pending, passed or failed > Closed [M6]. Manual review triggers: ACV over USD 1M, multiple partners on one referral, PAYG pricing [M6]. PAYG deals qualify if the same customer pays the same partner at least USD 25,000 in a year for the same solution; share before the first invoice; one-time TCV update allowed if TCV exceeds USD 1M within the same fiscal year [M6].

Exceptions: one per referral, within 60 days of initial registration, up to 5,000 characters, decided in up to 14 working days, final; fields like TCV, dates and perpetual flag can be reopened; "deployed on Azure", solution value, Marketplace flag and pricing model cannot (create a new referral and request an exception on sign date) [M6].

In P2P deals, only the partner holding the end-customer contract for the IP should register [M7]. Only IP incentives (Azure IP co-sell, Biz Apps premium and standard) are eligible for deal registration [M9]. ISV Connect (Business Applications) registration needs deal value over USD 5,000; bulk CSV upload up to 1,000 records [M6].

### 6.7 The seller engagement playbook that works

From Microsoft and practitioners (label noted):
1. **Be eligible before you ask.** Transactable, Azure IP co-sell eligible, MACC Enrolled; otherwise "go get that first; nothing below substitutes for it" [M89, VENDOR on Microsoft blog].
2. **Start with three to five deals** with a strong joint value proposition and a clear ask; after acceptance, send the seller a short note: the quota credit they will receive, the better-together story, and exactly what you need [M79].
3. **Make a specific request** ("introduce us to the AE at X by date Y"); generic "let's collaborate" asks go unactioned [M90].
4. **Write collateral for the seller, not the buyer:** elevator pitch, workload alignment, Marketplace path, commercial context; a bill of materials for Microsoft sellers [M79][M90].
5. **Qualify inbound referrals fast** with a named owner before mobilizing the team; Clazar recommends responding in under 4 hours (VENDOR best practice) [M90][M102].
6. **Run joint account planning early** (stakeholder map, Azure spend, MACC status, commercial path, named next steps) and bring CSAs or specialists in for technical validation; agree meeting roles (partner leads solution and value, Microsoft leads Azure context and commercial path) [M90].
7. **Run technical and commercial workstreams in parallel**; prepare the private offer during discovery, not closing week [M90].
8. **Ask discovery questions about MACC:** "Does your organization have a Microsoft Azure Consumption Commitment?" then position that an eligible Marketplace purchase counts toward it [M89].
9. **Use propensity scoring** (Marketplace Rewards and now FAM Differentiate benefit) to target MACC and Marketplace-buying accounts; channel partners at higher tiers also get it [M87][M93].
10. **Keep Partner Center current** after every stage, date or stakeholder change; use the PDM for escalations only [M90].
11. **Skilling:** Microsoft's five-part "Co-Sell Execution Engine" video series (Titan, Partner Skilling Hub, August 2026) covers fundamentals, account prioritization, referral quality and operations [M110]; Partner Center "MCEM for Partners" learning path explains stage alignment [M12].

Microsoft-published outcome claims: co-sell deals show 84 percent higher win rates and 41 percent larger deals, and deals shared with partners rose 55 percent year over year [M71]. Customer example: Contentsquare's Marketplace co-sell deals average 81 percent larger, close 11 percent faster, and a 106 percent increase in ACV versus direct new business [M79].

## 12. Strategic decision points (for Alex)

### 12.1 Should we invest in Microsoft this fiscal year?

Invest if two or more are true: (a) your product runs on Azure or you can deploy an Azure-hosted instance; (b) your ICP includes enterprises with Azure commitments (Microsoft says 85 percent of Marketplace buyers have them) [M94]; (c) you already have customers who would renew through Marketplace; (d) your buyers use Microsoft 365, Dynamics, Security or Fabric and your product integrates. Hold or treat as procurement-only if you are not Azure-hosted and have no Microsoft-integrated use case: without Azure IP co-sell eligibility there is no MACC drawdown and little seller motive [M1].

### 12.2 Which "tier" to chase in FY27

| Situation | Target for FY27 | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| New to Microsoft ISV | FAM no-cost, transactable listing, first MBS (Grow) | Unlocks GTM benefits and Azure sponsorship | [M26][M28] |
| Transacting, under USD 100K MBS | Azure IP co-sell eligible | MACC plus seller incentive plus deal registration | [M1] |
| USD 100K to 4M MBS | Grow tiers; build toward Azure CSD via 12 net-new customers over USD 10K or 30 transactions with 8 customers (cheaper than USD 4M) | CSD gates top sponsorship (up to USD 2.1M), CMMP (up to USD 275K), seller prioritization | [M59][M94][M28] |
| USD 4M+ MBS or big MACC customers | Azure CSD, Tiers 7 to 9 (USD 8M, 20M, 40M) | Maximum sponsorship, prioritization | [M26] |
| Services partner | One SPD plus two to three specializations tied to incentives and Cloud Accelerate Factory | Incentive eligibility and seller signal | [M13][M46] |
| MSP holding Azure Expert MSP | Frontier Partner specialization | AEMSP retiring | [M53] |

Premium FAM (USD 1,549.80 per year) is almost always worth it for an ISV in Build or early Grow: the USD 30K dev/test credit alone exceeds the fee, plus a named Engagement Manager [M32][M94]. Timing flip: if an ISV Success renewal is imminent, enrolling fresh in FAM gets the full USD 30K immediately, while migrated partners keep old credit tiers until renewal [M94].

### 12.3 Marketplace-first or not

In FY27 the default answer for Azure-hosted ISVs selling to enterprises is yes: co-sell credit flows through MBS, PRACR is retired as a broad mechanism, and MACC drawdown is uncapped [M13][M94]. The call flips when: customers are predominantly CSP-funded SMBs (use CSP private offers instead, no MACC) [M36]; you are not Azure-hosted [M1]; your contracts depend on hardware or bundled services that cannot be sold as software in Marketplace (MPO excludes professional services and hardware) [M39]; or procurement requires terms the Publisher Agreement forbids.

### 12.4 When private offers beat direct

Private offers beat direct paper when: the customer has MACC (100 percent drawdown turns spend into "already budgeted"); procurement wants consolidated Microsoft billing, PO mapping and existing agreement terms [M91]; you want 1.5 percent renewal economics [M42]; you need flexible billing or custom term lengths up to 10 years [M13]; or you want Azure sponsorship tied to the deal (only for Marketplace deals) [M31]. Direct still wins when the customer is CSP-only, when deal needs hardware or bundled services, or when the customer's billing account is outside supported MPO or REO countries (for channel deals).

### Runbook 5: Create a customer private offer

3. Marketplace offers > Private offers > Customers > + New private offer. Choose price adjustment on public plan or absolute price as a new SaaS or professional services plan (for 1 to 120 month terms). Done when: type chosen [M34][M35].
4. Enter billing account ID, accept-by date, start (accepted date or specific month), end date, renewal attestation (cannot be done later), up to 10 plans, pricing, flexible billing if needed, PDF terms (one, up to 3 MB), notification contacts. Done when: submitted [M39][M42].

6. Customer accepts in Azure portal > Private offer management, then purchases (subscribe or deploy) and configures SaaS within 30 days. Done when: purchase confirmation; SaaS activated [M39].
